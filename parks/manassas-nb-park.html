Park History

NPSHistory.com

Manassas National Battlefield Park in Prince William County, Virginia, preserves the site of two major Civil War battles. The First Battle of Bull Run (Manassas) on July 21, 1861, was the war's first major land engagement, shattering both sides' expectations of a quick conflict and earning Confederate General Thomas J. Jackson his famous nickname "Stonewall." The Second Battle of Manassas, fought August 28–30, 1862, was a decisive Confederate victory that brought Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia to the doorstep of Washington, D.C. and set the stage for Lee's first invasion of the North. The battlefield was designated a national battlefield park in 1940 and preserves approximately 5,000 acres of the historic landscape.

On July 21, 1861, Confederate forces defeated Union troops at the First Battle of Manassas, shocking Americans with the realization that civil war would be long and bloody.

The battle, also called Bull Run, convinced both North and South that a quick war was impossible. Confederate General Thomas J. Jackson earned his famous nickname 'Stonewall' Jackson when soldiers said he stood 'like a stone wall'. Established as Manassas National Battlefield Park in 1940, it has grown to encompass 5,100 acres.

Among the content targeted: “The War Over Memory” — language that the administration has flagged for review under its directive to review historically accurate interpretive materials.

Why this matters: Manassas shattered the North's hope for quick victory, transforming the Civil War into a grinding, four-year struggle for the nation's soul. Over 700,000 people visit this site each year.
