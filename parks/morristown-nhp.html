Park History

NPSHistory.com

Morristown National Historical Park in Morristown, New Jersey, was the first national historical park in the United States, established on March 2, 1933. The park preserves sites associated with two critical winter encampments of the Continental Army during the American Revolution. During the winter of 1779–1780, General George Washington quartered his army of approximately 10,000 soldiers at Jockey Hollow, where they endured what was recorded as the coldest winter of the 18th century — with 28 snowstorms and temperatures colder than at Valley Forge. Washington used the nearby Ford Mansion as his headquarters. The park also preserves Fort Nonsense and the New Jersey Brigade encampment area.

During the brutal winter of 1779-1780, General George Washington and 10,000 Continental Army soldiers encamped at Morristown, New Jersey, enduring what soldiers called the worst winter of the 18th century — colder even than Valley Forge.

Washington's army survived 28 snowstorms during the Morristown encampment, with snow drifts reaching six feet. Morristown was established as the first National Historical Park in 1933. The park preserves Washington's headquarters at the Ford Mansion and the Jockey Hollow encampment site.

For a new wayside not yet fabricated, suggested SO 3431 edits would change “The Ford family enslaved at least three people” to “The Ford family relied on at least three such individuals,” and “Their enslavers were Reverend John Mason and Captain John Johnson” to “They were owned by Reverend John Mason and Captain John Johnson.” [NPS review record]

Why this matters: Soldiers were not the only people who lived through that winter here. Hannah and Isaac Till, held in slavery by Reverend John Mason and Captain John Johnson, were rented by George Washington to work at the Ford Mansion, and the Ford family enslaved at least three people on this farm. The suggested edits keep the facts but trade “enslavers” for “owned by,” wording that puts the slaveholders’ claim of ownership ahead of the people held. [NPS review record]

What Was Flagged for Review

This is for a new wayside exhibit panel that has yet to be fabricated. To ensure the sign conforms to SO3431, the following text changes are suggested:

Existing Text: "It took a lot of paid servants and enslaved people to support a large household in the 1700s. The Ford family enslaved at least three people to provide labor for them on this farm. An unknown number of enslaved people worked in the Continental Army’s headquarters. Hannah and Isaac Till were two of them.

Their enslavers were Reverend John Mason and Captain John Johnson. General George Washington rented the couple from these men to work at the Ford Mansion.“

Revised Text: ”It took a number of paid servants and individuals held in slavery to support a large household in the 1700s. The Ford family relied on at least three such individuals to provide labor on this farm. An unknown number of people in bondage also worked at the Continental Army’s headquarters. Hannah and Isaac Till were among them.

They were owned by Reverend John Mason and Captain John Johnson. General George Washington arranged to rent the couple from these men to work at the Ford Mansion."
