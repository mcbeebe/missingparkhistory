Park History

NPSHistory.com

Harpers Ferry National Historical Park in West Virginia, Maryland, and Virginia preserves the town at the confluence of the Potomac and Shenandoah Rivers where abolitionist John Brown led his famous 1859 raid on the federal armory — a pivotal event that accelerated the nation's march toward civil war. Harpers Ferry changed hands eight times between Union and Confederate forces during the Civil War and witnessed the largest surrender of Union troops during the war in September 1862. After the war, Storer College — one of the first integrated schools in the United States — was established in the town to educate formerly enslaved people. The Niagara Movement, the precursor to the NAACP, held its second meeting at Storer College in 1906. The park was established in 1944.

The story of Harpers Ferry NHP is inseparable from the lives of the people who were enslaved here. For generations, their stories went untold. The interpretive materials now targeted for revision were created to ensure these voices would finally be heard.

The administration has ordered revised content that includes the language: “target practice.”, “They threw his body into the river and continued to fire shots at it.”, “a drunken mob murders”, and “Some townspeople, enraged by the mayor's death, kill William Thompson and toss his body into the Potomac.”.

Why this matters: The history of slavery at Harpers Ferry NHP was suppressed for generations. The materials now being targeted represent decades of work by historians, descendants of enslaved people, and community advocates to ensure these stories are finally told. Removing them would re-erase history that has only recently been acknowledged.
