Park History

NPSHistory.com

Vicksburg National Military Park in Vicksburg, Mississippi, preserves the site of the decisive Civil War campaign and 47-day siege that gave the Union control of the Mississippi River. After months of failed approaches, General Ulysses S. Grant executed a brilliant campaign in the spring of 1863, crossing the river south of Vicksburg, winning five battles in 17 days, and laying siege to the city's defenses. The Confederate garrison surrendered on July 4, 1863 — one day after the Union victory at Gettysburg — and President Abraham Lincoln declared that "The Father of Waters again goes unvexed to the sea." The park, established in 1899, contains over 1,340 monuments, markers, and plaques; a restored Union gunboat (the USS Cairo); and the Vicksburg National Cemetery with over 17,000 Union graves.

On May 18 - July 4, 1863, Union General Ulysses S. Grant besieged the fortress city of Vicksburg, Mississippi, in the Civil War's most decisive campaign.

Known as the 'Gibraltar of the Confederacy,' Vicksburg controlled the Mississippi River and was essential to Confederate supply lines. President Lincoln said 'Vicksburg is the key' to victory; the city's surrender gave the Union control of the entire Mississippi River. The 47-day siege and accompanying campaign resulted in over 40,000 total casualties and changed the war's trajectory.

Why this matters: Vicksburg marks the moment the Union gained control of the Mississippi River, effectively splitting the Confederacy in two. Over 500,000 people visit this site each year.
