Park History

NPSHistory.com

Petersburg National Battlefield in Virginia preserves the sites associated with the Siege of Petersburg, a nine-month campaign from June 1864 to April 1865 that ultimately led to the end of the Civil War. General Ulysses S. Grant's strategy to cut the vital railroad supply lines feeding the Confederate capital at Richmond resulted in one of the war's longest sustained operations, with miles of trenches foreshadowing the trench warfare of World War I. The siege included the Battle of the Crater on July 30, 1864, in which Union forces detonated a mine beneath Confederate lines — an assault that ended in a disastrous Union defeat and particularly heavy casualties among United States Colored Troops. The fall of Petersburg on April 2, 1865, led directly to the evacuation of Richmond and Lee's surrender at Appomattox a week later.

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW: Interpretive materials

Interpretive materials 0 photos

Petersburg National Battlefield in VA is one of hundreds of National Park Service sites where interpretive content has been flagged under Secretary's Order 3431. This directive requires parks to review materials that could be considered negative about past or living Americans — a standard that threatens the factual historical record these sites were created to preserve.

Why this matters: Petersburg National Battlefield is one of 466+ National Park Service sites where historically accurate interpretive content has been flagged under Secretary's Order 3431. Over 300 million people visit national parks each year. For many — especially schoolchildren — these visits are their first and deepest encounter with American history.
