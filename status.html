The cases Status pills updated weekly

Stonewall Pride flag · Gilbert Baker Foundation v. Interior

S.D.N.Y. · Lambda Legal
Won
Settled April 13, 2026. A court-enforceable agreement requires the Park Service to keep the Pride flag flying at Stonewall National Monument. The flag was back within a week. Still the only outright win against Secretary's Order 3431.

NPCA v. Department of the Interior

D. Mass. (Judge Kelley) · appeal in the First Circuit · Democracy Forward for six plaintiff groups
Injunction paused
June 12: nationwide order to restore removed materials within 21 days. July 2: the First Circuit stayed that order pending appeal, finding the groups had not shown irreparable harm to themselves. It did not rule on whether SO 3431 is lawful. August 7: Judge Kelley declined to freeze the trial-court schedule, so the case continues on the merits.
Next: First Circuit decision on the appeal; summary-judgment briefing in Boston.

City of Philadelphia v. Burgum · President's House

E.D. Pa. (Judge Rufe) · Third Circuit
Reversed
June 18: a unanimous Third Circuit panel threw out the February order that had restored the slavery panels. July 13: four of the city's five claims dismissed. July 14–15: the administration installed its own panels overnight, moving the stories of the nine people Washington enslaved to the rear of the site. August 18: rehearing denied.
Left: a petition to the Supreme Court, and one breach-of-agreement claim still alive in the trial court.

Where the Fight Stands

The Interior Department has disapproved more than 130 partner-run park projects worth at least $25 million as misaligned with administration priorities, internal National Park Service records obtained by KUNC revealed on September 30. The blocked agreements include cultural-resource work at Death Valley and museum digitization at Zion, with the Great Basin Institute alone having 78 projects worth $12.7 million disapproved or left in limbo. The systematic obstruction of third-party conservation work compounds the agency’s operational collapse, coming just weeks after a CNN investigation documented that NPS has lost more than 24% of its permanent workforce since January 2025, even as Congress rejected the administration’s proposed $1.2 billion cut—a historic 37% decrease—and provided only a 2% reduction. The administration’s spending decisions have not translated into staffing or partner support, leaving parks struggling with severe personnel shortages as ideological directives choke off collaborative research and preservation.

The partner-project blockade intensifies ongoing battles over interpretive content, as librarians and archivists race to digitally preserve national park signs about women and LGBTQ+ history that have been revised or destroyed under administration orders, including language describing a massacre of Blackfeet women and children at Grand Teton National Park. The Interior Department removed climate change signage from Acadia National Park in September 2025, with spokesperson Aubrie Spady dismissing the removed materials as “brainless fear-mongering rhetoric” rather than “real science.” Meanwhile, a coalition including NPCA, DC Preservation League, and the National Mall Coalition filed for a preliminary injunction in early September to halt unauthorized construction at West Potomac Park, where the administration seeks to transform park land into a massive sculpture garden complex. The Sierra Club obtained documents on September 23 revealing a secretive reorganization plan that would strip authority from experienced park officials, suggesting the workforce and partnership crises are features of a broader strategy to hollow out the agency’s institutional capacity and reshape what the Park Service protects and tells.
