Ways to help

Four fronts. Pick yours.

The fight over park history is happening in Congress, in federal courtrooms, on trails and in visitor centers, and online. Each one has a job for you. Not sure where things stand? Read the status report first.

In Congress

Cut off the money

Congress funds the Interior Department. A line in the FY2027 bill can bar any dollar from implementing Secretary's Order 3431. Fifty-three House members and two senators have already asked for it.

  • Enter your ZIP and we draft the letter to your members. Edit it, copy it, or text it with Resistbot.
  • Ask them to cosponsor the Truth in National Parks Act (H.R. 8539).
  • Write a letter to the editor of your local paper.
In the courts

Back the people suing

The nationwide case is on appeal; Philadelphia's is nearly out of road. Litigation is slow and expensive, and these groups are carrying it.

  • NPCA and Democracy Forward, lead plaintiff and counsel in NPCA v. Interior.
  • PEER and the Sierra Club, suing for the records that show what was removed and why.
  • Lambda Legal, which won the Stonewall flag settlement.
Donate to NPCA's Park Protection Fund
In the parks

Document what is there

The government's own list of removals is "unlikely to capture every removal," an official admitted in court. Volunteers' photographs are the record.

  • Photograph every sign at a park Save Our Signs lists as under-covered. More than 14,700 photos from 412 sites so far; the gaps are mapped.
  • See a blank wall or a replacement panel? Report it to Erasure in Action and tell us so the map stays right.
  • Join the volunteer monitor network.

Online

Make it impossible to do quietly

Removals depend on nobody noticing. Every share, embed and subscriber raises the cost of the next one.

  • Embed the map on your site, newsletter or class page. It is free.
  • Share a park page when the news hits that park.
  • Use NPCA's social toolkit for ready-made posts.
